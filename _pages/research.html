An Introduction to Circular Restricted Three-Body Problem: CR3BP

  • Abstract: This project studies the dynamics of a restricted circular restricted three-body problem, CR3BP. Motivated by the closed solution of a two-body problem, an additional massless object is added into the system, and we derive the set of Lagrange points of the third body as well as their corresponding orbits and stability.

Figure 1: In a restricted planar circular three-body problem, we assume two bodies m1 > m2 together with a massless object, for example the Sun-Earth-Satellite system. In this system, there are five different Lagrange points for the third body, satellite: three collinear Lagrange points and two equilateral Lagrange points. If m2 ≤ 0.04m1, then we would achieve a steady state around the equilateral Lagrange points L4,L5.

Figure 2: This type of stability behavior has been observed in the Solar System. For instance, there is a sub-class of asteroids, known as the Trojan asteroids, which are trapped in the vicinity of the L4 and L5 points of the Sun-Jupiter system, and consequently share Jupiter's orbit around the Sun, staying approximately 60 degrees ahead of and 60 degrees behind Jupiter, respectively.

Talks and Conferences

The Seminars in Undergraduate Mathematics in Montreal (SUMM), 2026

  • Topic: Statistical Inference for Survival Data.
  • Abstract: Survival analysis studies the behavior of time-to-event data, which arise naturally in modern statistical studies and are characterized by incomplete observations due to censoring and truncation. These unique features invalidate classical statistical methods and require the development of specialized inferential tools. This talk presents an introduction to basic theories and methodologies used for modeling time-to-event data, which are widely used in biostatistical and epidemiological research. We introduce the censoring and truncation mechanism, followed by the famous Kaplan-Meier, KM, estimator and its variants. If time permits, we will also talk about the Cox proportional hazards, PH, model and regression techniques.
